# Stock Market Fundamentals — Summary

## 1. What Is the Stock Market?
The stock market is a place where investors buy and sell shares of listed companies. It helps companies raise capital and allows investors to participate in business ownership.

## 2. NSE and BSE
NSE (National Stock Exchange) and BSE (BSE Limited) are major stock exchanges in India. They provide electronic platforms for trading securities.

## 3. Nifty and Sensex
Nifty 50 tracks 50 major companies listed on the NSE. The Sensex tracks 30 major companies listed on the BSE. Both indices help measure overall market performance.

## 4. IPO and SME IPO
An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is when a company offers its shares to the public for the first time. An SME IPO is designed for eligible small and medium-sized enterprises under specific exchange requirements.

## 5. Market Capitalization
Market capitalization is the total market value of a company's outstanding shares.

Formula: Market Capitalization = Share Price × Outstanding Shares.

## 6. PE Ratio, PB Ratio, and EPS
- PE Ratio: Share price divided by earnings per share (EPS).
- PB Ratio: Share price divided by book value per share.
- EPS: A company's earnings attributable to each outstanding share, calculated using the applicable weighted-average share count.

These measures help analysts evaluate a company's valuation and financial performance.

## 7. Dividend, Bonus, and Stock Split
A dividend is a distribution of profits to shareholders, subject to company decisions and applicable rules. Bonus shares are additional shares issued to existing shareholders. A stock split increases the number of shares and reduces the face value per share proportionately; by itself, it does not increase the investor's total investment value.

## 8. Trading Volume
Trading volume is the number of shares traded during a specified period. It helps analysts understand trading activity and market participation.

## 9. Financial Statements
- Balance Sheet: Shows assets, liabilities, and equity at a particular date.
- Profit and Loss Statement: Shows income, expenses, and profit or loss over a period.
- Cash Flow Statement: Shows cash inflows and outflows from operating, investing, and financing activities.

## 10. Basic Financial Ratios
Financial ratios help assess profitability, liquidity, efficiency, and financial risk. Examples include return on equity (ROE), return on assets (ROA), and debt-to-equity ratio. Banks require additional sector-specific measures, such as net interest margin (NIM), gross and net non-performing assets (GNPA/NNPA), and capital adequacy.

## Conclusion
Stock market knowledge helps data analysts interpret financial data, understand market movements, compare companies, and prepare meaningful business reports. Financial analysis supports informed decisions but does not guarantee future performance.
